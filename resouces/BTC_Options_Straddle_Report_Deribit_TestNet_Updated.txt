BTC OPTIONS SHORT STRADDLE

Assignment-Focused Implementation Report

NautilusTrader + Deribit TestNet

DETAILED PROBLEM STATEMENT

The task is to build and execute a Bitcoin (BTC) at-the-money (ATM)
short straddle using NautilusTrader connected to the Deribit exchange
through its TestNet environment. The strategy must identify a valid BTC
options Call and Put contract that share exactly the same strike price
and expiry, then sell one Call and one Put to create the short straddle.
The complete workflow must cover trading-system initialization, exchange
connectivity, option-market-data subscriptions, instrument discovery,
ATM contract selection, order submission, exchange acknowledgements,
executions, and open-position tracking.

Level 1 — Core short-straddle execution

Set up and initialize NautilusTrader, configure the Deribit integration,
and connect to Deribit TestNet.

Subscribe to BTC options market data and discover the BTC option
instruments available for trading.

Obtain the BTC underlying reference price and compare it with available
option strikes to determine the nearest ATM strike.

Select the matching ATM Call and ATM Put. Both contracts must have
exactly the same strike price and expiry, and must be active, valid, and
tradable.

Submit SELL orders for one Call and one Put. Track each leg
independently through submission, acknowledgement/acceptance, partial
execution, and final execution.

Track resulting open positions using actual filled quantities and fill
prices. Submission or acknowledgement alone must never be treated as an
executed trade.

Level 2 — Combined-premium risk management

Extend Level 1 with continuous monitoring of the combined premium
required to buy back the open Call and Put positions. Calculate buyback
value using actual filled open quantities and current ask-side option
quotes, applying contract multipliers and currency/denomination
conversions consistently where required. Trigger a stop-loss when the
combined buyback premium increases by 1% from the combined entry
premium. Trigger a take-profit when the combined buyback premium
decreases by 2% from the combined entry premium. When either condition
is met, submit BUY-to-close orders for both open legs and track
acknowledgements and executions until the positions are closed. Level 1
itself must not contain stop-loss, take-profit, or automated exit
conditions; these are Level 2 requirements only.

Engineering and execution requirements

The implementation must explain NautilusTrader initialization, Deribit
configuration, market-data subscriptions and events, instrument
discovery, ATM contract selection, order submission, order-state
transitions, execution reports, and position tracking. It must also
explain Level 2 threshold calculations and monitoring, partial-fill
handling, and recovery when only one leg fills or the two legs fill in
different quantities. Call and Put legs must be managed independently.
Any unmatched exposure must be identified and handled safely; it must
not be treated as a completed straddle. The system must prevent
duplicate exit attempts and must not calculate risk using missing,
stale, or invalid quotes.

Expected outcome and scope

The expected outcome is an explainable TestNet demonstration of the BTC
ATM short straddle, with Level 1 execution and Level 2 risk controls
clearly separated. The candidate should be able to describe the
implementation, show order and position evidence, explain incomplete
parts or errors, and discuss debugging steps taken. This is an
exchange-connected options execution exercise; it does not require a
price-prediction model, AI/ML forecasting system, historical research
pipeline, or production live-money trading service. Development and
demonstration must use Deribit TestNet. A short straddle can incur
substantial losses if BTC moves sharply; the specified thresholds are
execution rules, not guarantees against loss or slippage.

2.  Short-Straddle Fundamentals

A short straddle consists of selling a Call option and selling a Put
option on the same underlying, with the same strike price and expiry. In
this assignment, both contracts are BTC options.

Short Call: the strategy receives a premium for selling the Call and may
be required to buy it back or settle according to the contract.

Short Put: the strategy receives a premium for selling the Put and may
be required to buy it back or settle according to the contract.

ATM: the strike closest to the selected BTC underlying reference price.

Combined entry premium: the total premium actually received from the
filled Call and Put quantities, calculated in consistent units.

Buyback premium: the current estimated cost to close the short
positions. For Level 2, use the ask-side quotes as required by the
assignment.

2.1 Basic payoff intuition

The position benefits when the combined value of the two options falls
after entry, because the options can potentially be bought back for less
than the premium received. A sharp move in BTC can increase the value of
one option and create losses. This report does not assume a guaranteed
profit or a maximum loss for the short straddle.

4.2 Market-data module: what data/market_data.py will do

1.  Connect to the Deribit TestNet market-data service through the

2.  Discover/load the available BTC option instrument definitions and

small mocked data fixtures may still be used in unit tests.

Implementation boundary: data/market_data.py should retrieve and
normalize market data, not decide which strike to trade and not submit
orders. ATM selection belongs in strategies/atm_selection.py; order
submission belongs in execution/order_manager.py; Level 2 threshold

6.  BTC Option Discovery and ATM Contract Selection

The strategy must identify an eligible Call and Put pair. Use the
following deterministic selection procedure:

Obtain the BTC reference price from the documented source.

Load the available BTC option instrument definitions.

Filter to instruments for the intended underlying and supported option
category.

Separate Call and Put instruments and extract their strike and expiry
metadata.

Identify strike values available for both a Call and a Put at a common
expiry.

For each eligible common strike, calculate the absolute distance from
the BTC reference price.

Choose the strike with the smallest absolute distance; this is the
nearest ATM strike.

Choose the expiry using a documented deterministic policy, then select
the Call and Put with exactly the same strike and expiry.

Validate instrument status, quote availability, lot size, minimum
quantity, tick size and contract multiplier before submitting orders.

6.1 Selection formula

ATM strike = argminₖ |K − BTC reference price|

Here, K represents an eligible strike for which both Call and Put
contracts exist at the selected expiry. If two strikes are equally
close, define a consistent tie-breaking rule in the configuration or
code and log the selected result.

7.  Level 1 — Short-Straddle Execution

Level 1 is the minimum required working strategy.

Complete the instrument selection and validate both option contracts.

Determine the configured order quantity and verify it is valid for each
instrument.

Submit a SELL order for the selected ATM Call.

Submit a SELL order for the selected ATM Put.

Track each order independently through submitted, accepted, rejected,
partially filled and filled states, as applicable.

Record actual execution prices and quantities for both legs.

Confirm the resulting open positions using NautilusTrader’s position
state.

Continue observing market data and log position and market changes.

7.1 Entry premium accounting

Calculate entry premium from actual fills, not requested order prices.
If an order is filled in multiple executions, aggregate the execution
values using the actual filled quantities and the contract’s documented
multiplier and currency conventions. Keep Call and Put accounting
separate before calculating the combined total.

Use the exchange and instrument specifications to determine whether the
premium is quoted per unit, per contract or in another convention. Do
not assume the Call and Put quantities or multipliers are
interchangeable without checking the instrument definitions.

8.  Order and Position Lifecycle

Order status and position status are different. A successful order
submission does not prove that the order was accepted or filled. The
strategy must use execution events and position state as the source of
truth.

9.  Level 2 — Automated Risk Management

Level 2 extends the Level 1 strategy. It continuously monitors the
combined premium required to buy back both short options. The assignment
specifies a 1% stop-loss and a 2% take-profit.

9.1 Calculation

Let:

E = combined entry premium received for the actual filled quantities of
both legs.

B = current combined buyback cost, calculated using ask-side quotes and
the actual open quantities.

Stop-loss: B ≥ E × 1.01

Take-profit: B ≤ E × 0.98

Calculate B using the current ask-side quote for each leg, multiplied by
the actual quantity still open and the correct contract multiplier or
unit conversion. Ensure E and B use the same currency and valuation
convention. If a leg has no open quantity, its buyback contribution is
zero; do not include quantities that have already been closed.

9.2 Exit execution

Check that both leg quotes are valid and sufficiently fresh for
valuation.

Evaluate the combined buyback cost against the configured thresholds.

When a threshold is crossed, set an exit-in-progress state before
submitting closing orders.

Submit BUY orders only for the remaining open short quantities of each
leg.

Track closing order acknowledgements, partial fills, rejections and
final fills.

Reconcile actual positions after execution and confirm that both legs
are flat.

Do not submit another exit set while an exit is already in progress;
handle retries only after reconciling current order and position state.

10. Partial Fills and Unmatched-Leg Handling

The two legs are separate orders and may not execute at the same time or
in the same quantity. The strategy must not assume that submitting both
orders creates an atomic two-leg trade.

10.1 Entry recovery procedure

Track the Call and Put order and fill quantities independently.

If one leg fills while the other is pending, continue monitoring the
pending order for a configured, documented period or event condition.

If the second leg is rejected, cancelled or remains unfilled beyond the
configured policy, stop treating the pair as a completed straddle.

Apply a predefined recovery action: cancel remaining unfilled quantity
and close the filled exposure, or use another explicitly approved
recovery procedure.

Verify the final position state and record the reason and actions taken.

For a safe first implementation, use a conservative recovery policy that
closes the filled leg if the matching leg cannot be established. The
policy must account for partial quantities and must not over-close a
position.

10.2 Exit recovery procedure

If one closing order fills and the other does not, keep the exit state
active and continue managing the remaining open exposure.

If a closing order is rejected or partially filled, reconcile the actual
open quantity before retrying.

Never send a closing order for more than the remaining open position.

Verify both positions are flat before marking the straddle as closed.

Phase 4 — Select the ATM Call and Put with matching expiry

Objective: Select one eligible Call and one eligible Put that have the
same strike and expiry, with the strike closest to the BTC reference
price.

Recommended method

Use a deterministic ATM-selection algorithm over the live instrument
catalogue. Apply eligibility checks before ranking. If multiple expiries
are listed, choose the expiry using a documented assignment-compatible
policy, such as the nearest eligible future expiry with sufficient time
remaining and available quotes. The precise expiry rule must be
configured and logged rather than left implicit.

Detailed implementation steps

Capture one BTC reference price with its timestamp and source.

Build the eligible Call set and eligible Put set from the discovered
instruments. Keep only future expiries and active/tradable contracts.

Create matched pairs by joining Call and Put instruments on exactly the
same strike and expiry.

Remove pairs where either leg has missing/stale quotes, invalid tick/lot
metadata, or fails minimum quantity checks.

Apply the configured expiry policy to choose the expiry group. Do not
compare a Call from one expiry with a Put from another.

Within the selected expiry group, calculate the absolute distance
between each available strike and the BTC reference price.

Choose the strike with the smallest absolute distance. If two strikes
are equally close, apply a deterministic tie-break rule (for example,
lower strike) and log it.

Select the Call and Put instruments for that exact strike and expiry.

Run a final pre-trade validation and store the selected pair as one
strategy candidate.

Core implementation logic

ATM selection formula: K* = argmin_K |K - S|, where S is the BTC
reference price and K is an available strike.

Pair constraint: Call.strike = Put.strike and Call.expiry = Put.expiry.

Eligibility constraint: both instruments must be active, future-dated,
supported by the venue, and pass quote and quantity checks.

Do not choose the Call and Put independently by nearest strike; pair
them first on identical strike and expiry.

Record reference price, reference timestamp, candidate strikes, chosen
expiry, chosen strike and rejection reasons.

Inputs and configuration

BTC reference price and timestamp.

Option instrument catalogue and parsed option metadata.

Configured expiry policy, quote-freshness limit and desired order
quantity.

Venue-provided tick size, lot size, minimum quantity and contract
multiplier.

Expected output / completion evidence

Exactly one selected Call and one selected Put are produced.

Both legs have identical strike and expiry.

The selected strike is the closest eligible strike to the BTC reference
price, subject to the documented tie-break.

A selection report/log explains why this pair was chosen.

Testing and acceptance criteria

Use a small mock catalogue to test ATM selection with a strike exactly
at BTC price.

Test BTC price between two strikes and confirm the nearest strike wins.

Test equal-distance strikes and confirm deterministic tie-break.

Test mismatched expiry and missing-leg cases; no invalid pair should be
returned.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not hardcode one strike or one expiry.

Do not pair contracts that merely have similar expiry labels; compare
normalized expiry values.

Do not proceed if only one side of the straddle is available.

Phase 6 — Order lifecycle, positions and unmatched-leg recovery

Objective: Maintain an accurate view of order status and actual
positions for each option leg, and handle partial fills, rejection,

Recommended method

Use NautilusTrader order/position events as the primary event stream and

Detailed implementation steps

Create an order-event handler that maps each event to the relevant Call
or Put leg using the client order ID and instrument ID.

Update cumulative filled quantity using fill events; do not overwrite
cumulative quantity with the latest partial fill quantity.

Track open position quantity and average fill price for each instrument

Write a concise event log with timestamps, instrument IDs, order IDs,

Phase 7 — Level 2: combined-premium stop-loss and take-profit

Objective: Monitor the combined cost of buying back the actual open Call
and Put quantities and close both legs when the assignment’s 1%
stop-loss or 2% take-profit threshold is reached.

Recommended method

Implement one combined risk monitor that evaluates both legs from
current executable ask-side quotes. Calculate the entry reference from
actual fills and the live buyback value from actual remaining short
quantities. Use consistent units, multipliers and currencies throughout.

Detailed implementation steps

Enable this phase only after Level 1 order and position tracking is
reliable.

At entry completion, calculate and store the combined entry premium E
from actual Call and Put fills. If the legs fill at different times or
in different quantities, calculate the reference using the actual filled
quantities and the chosen documented aggregation convention.

On each valid market-data update or risk-monitor cycle, retrieve the
latest ask quote for each short option leg.

Check quote freshness, positive price, available size where provided,
instrument multiplier and currency consistency. If a quote is invalid or
stale, mark the risk valuation unavailable and do not interpret it as
zero.

Calculate the estimated buyback cost B for each leg using its ask-side
quote, actual open quantity and contract multiplier; combine the two leg
values in a common currency.

Calculate stop-loss threshold SL = E × 1.01 and take-profit threshold
TP = E × 0.98.

If B >= SL, trigger the stop-loss. If B <= TP, trigger the take-profit.
If neither is true, continue monitoring.

When a threshold is triggered, atomically change strategy state to
EXITING before sending any close order. This prevents duplicate exit
attempts from repeated market-data events.

Cancel any outstanding entry orders and submit buy-to-close orders for
the actual open quantity of each short leg.

Track close orders and partial fills. Recalculate residual open
quantities and submit only the remaining quantity if the documented
order policy allows it.

Verify both positions are flat from execution events and position
reconciliation. Record trigger reason, threshold, observed buyback
value, quotes and final execution prices.

After a completed exit, mark the strategy cycle CLOSED and require a
fresh explicit start condition before opening another straddle.

Core implementation logic

E is the combined entry premium received for the actual filled
quantities, converted to one consistent currency and unit.

B is the estimated combined cost to buy back the actual currently open
quantities, using ask-side prices because the strategy must buy to close
short options.

Stop-loss: B >= E × 1.01 (buyback cost has increased by 1% from entry
reference).

Take-profit: B <= E × 0.98 (buyback cost has decreased by 2% from entry
reference).

Apply the contract multiplier and quantity correctly. Do not compare a
per-contract premium with a total-position premium.

Use a consistent definition for fees: if entry premium excludes fees,
the trigger reference should also exclude fees; separately report
estimated/realized fees. Document the convention.

Use a single exit lock/state transition to prevent duplicate close
orders.

Risk monitoring must not run on stale, missing or invalid quotes. Log
the valuation as unavailable and apply the configured safe behavior.

Inputs and configuration

Actual entry fills for both legs and their quantities.

Current open quantity for each leg.

Fresh ask-side quotes for both options.

Instrument multipliers and currency/settlement metadata.

Configured thresholds: 1% stop-loss and 2% take-profit.

Expected output / completion evidence

Combined entry reference E is stored and auditable.

Combined buyback estimate B is calculated from fresh ask-side quotes and
actual open quantities.

Stop-loss and take-profit conditions are evaluated exactly as defined.

Only one exit sequence is initiated for a trigger.

Both legs are bought back and confirmed flat, or the system reports the
remaining exposure and error clearly.

Testing and acceptance criteria

Unit test values: if E = 100, stop-loss threshold is 101 and take-profit
threshold is 98.

Test B = 100.99 (no stop-loss), B = 101 (stop-loss triggers), B = 98
(take-profit triggers), and values between thresholds.

Test one leg quote stale or missing; valuation must be unavailable
rather than zero.

Test partial close on one leg and verify only the residual open quantity
is closed.

Test repeated threshold events; only one exit sequence should be
submitted.

Test that a completed exit is verified by zero open quantities for both
instruments.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not reverse the comparisons: the stop-loss is triggered when buyback
cost rises; take-profit when it falls.

Do not use bid quotes to value a buy-to-close order; use ask-side
quotes.

Do not multiply quantity or contract multiplier twice.

Do not use requested order prices as actual exit results.

Do not claim that a trigger guarantees the exact fill price; market
conditions and slippage can change execution.

18. Improved Event-Driven Market-Data and Execution Flow

This section makes the event flow explicit because the engineering team
may ask how market data reaches the strategy, how orders are
acknowledged, and how fills become positions. NautilusTrader is
event-driven: the strategy should react to the events delivered by the
configured data and execution clients instead of repeatedly assuming
that a request succeeded.

18.1 Market-data event path

Subscribe to the BTC reference-price source and the selected option
instruments using the APIs supported by the installed NautilusTrader
version.

When a BTC reference update arrives, store its value, source and event
timestamp. The ATM selector uses this value together with the currently
available strikes.

When an option quote update arrives, update the corresponding
instrument’s bid, ask, sizes and timestamp. Keep Call and Put quote
state separate.

Reject or mark unavailable any quote that is stale, missing, crossed,
non-positive or lacks the data needed for the configured valuation.

Do not calculate buyback value from a last-traded price when the
assignment specifically requires ask-side quotes.

18.2 Order and execution event path

Order submitted means the application sent the request; it does not
prove the exchange accepted it.

Order accepted means the venue acknowledged the order; it does not prove
any quantity has executed.

A fill event reports an executed quantity and price. Accumulate fills
without counting duplicate events twice.

The position tracker must use actual executed quantities and the
venue/platform position state, not the order’s requested quantity.

On reconnect or restart, reconcile outstanding orders and positions with
the venue before permitting another entry.

19. Matched-Quantity Accounting and Partial-Fill Rules

The assignment requires combined premium valuation using both legs with
consistent contract quantities. This is especially important when the
Call and Put fill at different times or in different quantities.

19.1 Entry quantities

If qC equals qP, the full open quantity is matched.

If qC and qP differ, only the smaller common quantity is a matched
straddle. The excess quantity on the other leg is unmatched directional
exposure.

Do not label the strategy fully established while unmatched exposure
remains. Apply the documented recovery policy: cancel remaining entry
orders and close or otherwise safely resolve the unmatched residual.

For the assignment’s combined-straddle risk calculation, compare
like-for-like quantities on both legs. Do not add a full Call position
to a smaller Put position and describe the result as a balanced
straddle.

19.2 Combined entry premium and buyback value

If there are multiple fills at different prices, calculate a
quantity-weighted average fill price for each leg, or sum the individual
fill values directly. The method must preserve the same quantity basis
for E and B. Fees should be reported separately or included consistently
in both the entry and exit comparison; do not mix conventions.

19.3 Example: unequal fills

In this example, two Call contracts and two Put contracts form the
matched straddle. One extra short Call remains unmatched and must be
handled by the recovery procedure. It must not be ignored or hidden
inside the combined premium.

19.4 Rebase the risk reference after partial exits

Critical rule: the entry reference and current buyback value must always
use the same remaining matched quantity. If part of either leg has been
closed, do not compare the residual position’s buyback cost with the
original full-size entry premium. Recalculate the matched residual
quantity and the entry-premium basis attributable to that quantity. Any
unmatched residual is separate directional exposure and must be handled
by the recovery policy.

Let qC and qP be the current open short quantities and qM = min(qC, qP).
Allocate the original Call and Put entry fills to the remaining matched
quantity using the original fill prices and contract multipliers.
Calculate E_remaining as the entry premium attributable to qM on both
legs. Calculate B_remaining from current ask-side buyback values for qM
on both legs. Compare B_remaining with E_remaining × 1.01 and
E_remaining × 0.98. If account netting or position aggregation changes
the apparent quantities, reconcile the execution ledger and venue
position before evaluating the trigger.

Do not simply use min(qC, qP) with an unadjusted original E after
quantities have changed. Store closed quantities, close fills and
remaining entry basis per leg. Do not assume a partial close occurred at
the entry price.

qM = min(current_open_call_qty, current_open_put_qty) E_remaining =
entry_value_of_remaining_matched_call +
entry_value_of_remaining_matched_put B_remaining =
ask_buyback_value_for_qM_call + ask_buyback_value_for_qM_put SL =
E_remaining × 1.01 TP = E_remaining × 0.98

Evaluate only when both matched legs have valid fresh ask quotes and
consistent currency/contract units.

20. Profit-Aware Execution Quality: Assignment-Compliant Approach

There is no guaranteed highest-profit short-straddle configuration. The
assignment fixes the core strategy: sell the nearest ATM Call and Put
with the same strike and expiry. The improvements below aim to avoid
poor entries, reduce avoidable execution costs and improve the quality
of the trade without changing that required strategy.

20.1 Keep the required strategy unchanged

The selected strike must remain the nearest eligible strike to the BTC
reference price. Do not move to a farther strike merely because it
offers a larger premium.

The Call and Put must have exactly the same strike and expiry.

Level 1 must remain free of automated stop-loss, take-profit and exit
conditions, as stated in the assignment.

Level 2 must use the specified 1% stop-loss and 2% take-profit based on
the combined entry premium and combined buyback value.

20.2 Pre-trade quality filters

Apply these as safety and execution-quality checks to the required ATM
pair. If the nearest ATM pair fails a check, do not silently substitute
a different strike. Wait for valid data, refresh the pair, or report
that no trade is currently suitable.

20.3 Entry execution method

Prefer a controlled limit-order policy where supported. A sell limit
sets the minimum acceptable premium, but it may not fill; a market order
may fill faster but can suffer slippage.

Use a documented price policy based on the live bid/ask and the desired
execution urgency. Do not claim the midpoint is an executable price.

Set a bounded order timeout and a clear cancel/replace policy. After
cancellation, wait for the final order state and reconcile any fills
before replacing an order.

Coordinate the two legs with a defined two-leg execution policy. If one
leg fills and the other does not within the allowed window, cancel the
remainder and apply the unmatched-leg recovery procedure.

Record the decision inputs: reference price, chosen strike/expiry, both
bid/ask quotes, spread, quote timestamps, order type, limit price,
quantity and reason for accepting the entry.

20.4 Premium and risk discipline

Use actual fill prices and actual filled quantities for entry premium. A
quoted premium or submitted limit price is not realized credit.

Use ask-side quotes to estimate the cost of buying back short options,
exactly as the assignment specifies.

Apply the contract multiplier and quote currency exactly once. Convert
both legs to a common unit before adding them.

Keep Level 2 thresholds fixed at 1% and 2% for assignment compliance. Do
not widen the stop-loss or remove it to make a backtest appear more
profitable.

Include fees and realistic slippage in performance reporting. A gross
premium gain can become a net loss after execution costs.

Do not increase contract quantity or leverage simply to increase the
displayed premium. Size must remain within the configured test quantity
and account risk limits.

20.5 What makes this approach stronger

This is a quality-control and execution approach, not a claim of maximum
profitability. A short straddle has significant downside risk: losses
can become very large when BTC rises sharply, and a large fall can also
create substantial losses. Premium received is not guaranteed profit.

events, not by submission requests alone.

Quality check | Method | Reason

Quote freshness | Require recent bid/ask updates for both legs within a
configured age limit. | Avoid trading on stale prices.

Bid-ask spread | Calculate spread and spread as a proportion of the
midpoint; reject abnormally wide markets using a documented threshold. |
Reduce hidden entry and exit costs.

Available quote size | Check displayed size against the intended order
size where the feed provides it. | Reduce avoidable partial fills and
market impact.

Tradability | Confirm active instrument, future expiry, minimum
quantity, lot size and tick size. | Avoid rejected or invalid orders.

Pair integrity | Confirm same underlying, strike, expiry and compatible
contract specifications. | Ensure it is the required straddle.

Estimated net credit | Estimate sell-side entry proceeds from executable
bid-side prices, then account for expected fees and spread costs. |
Understand the realistic premium rather than relying on midpoint marks.

Account readiness | Check connection, open orders, current positions and
available margin/risk information where exposed. | Avoid duplicate
entries or preventable rejections.

Approach | Potential benefit | Trade-off / limitation

Nearest-ATM pair with strict validation | Maintains assignment
compliance and avoids invalid contract pairing. | May skip a trade if
the required pair is illiquid.

Spread and quote-size filters | Avoids entering when execution costs or
liquidity are poor. | Fewer trade opportunities.

Controlled limit execution | Can reduce slippage and set a minimum
acceptable credit. | Orders may remain unfilled or only partially fill.

Matched-quantity monitoring | Measures the intended paired exposure
correctly. | Requires careful partial-fill and recovery logic.

Fixed assignment SL/TP | Makes Level 2 behavior clear, testable and
reviewable. | Does not guarantee a capped loss or exact exit price
during fast markets.

State | Meaning | Allowed next action / transition

IDLE | No active strategy, unresolved order or position. | Validate
account and market data; move to READY.

READY | Pre-trade checks passed and a matched ATM pair is selected. |
Submit entry orders; move to ENTRY_WORKING.

ENTRY_WORKING | One or both entry orders are pending. | Process
acknowledgements and fills; block duplicate entry.

OPEN | Both legs have equal matched open quantities and no unresolved
entry orders. | Level 1: monitor only. Level 2: monitor combined buyback
value.

PARTIALLY_OPEN | Entry has partially filled or leg quantities are
unequal. | Cancel/reconcile remaining entry orders and resolve residual
exposure.

UNMATCHED_EXPOSURE | One leg has excess open quantity or the other leg
is missing. | Close/reduce residual under recovery policy; block new
entries.

RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED | Local state differs from venue state or a
reconnect/restart occurred. | Pause new entries; query venue orders and
positions and reconcile.

EXITING | A Level 2 trigger or recovery action has initiated close
orders. | Lock duplicate triggers and track close fills.

EXIT_PARTIALLY_FILLED | One or both close orders are partially executed.
| Recalculate residual open quantities and close only verified
residuals.

CLOSED | Both legs have zero open quantity and no relevant working
orders. | Archive cycle; permit a new entry only after fresh validation.

ERROR | An unresolved or non-recoverable failure occurred. | Block new
entries and require controlled recovery/manual review.
